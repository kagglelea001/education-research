**Beyond the Flagship: Spillover Effects of Global University Rankings on National Higher Education Systems**

**Abstract**

Governments worldwide invest billions in World-Class University (WCU) initiatives, yet causal evidence on their system-wide benefits remains scarce. This study provides the first quasi-experimental evidence on how flagship universities' global rankings generate spillover effects on non-flagship institutions within national higher education systems. Exploiting a regression discontinuity design around the QS World University Rankings Top 100 threshold across 54 countries, we analyze 532 spillover universities across nine outcome dimensions. McCrary tests confirm no manipulation (11.1%), validating the identification strategy. Results reveal significant positive spillovers on market-facing outcomes: international student enrollment increases by 28–39 points (85–145%, p<0.0001), employer reputation improves by 9–12 points (30–40%, p<0.005), faculty-student ratios rise by 33–42 points (83–102%, p<0.0001), and employment outcomes improve by 16 points (47%, p<0.001). Conversely, we document negative spillovers on research concentration metrics: citation rates decline by 29–39 points (74–119%, p<0.0001), sustainability scores fall by 21–25 points (54–61%, p<0.007), and international research network scores decrease by 17 points (24%, p<0.001). Of 45 estimates, 24 are significant (p<0.05): 17 positive and 7 negative. These findings suggest a brand-effect mechanism (DiMaggio & Powell, 1983) whereby ranking proximity enhances market signaling for non-flagship universities, while resource concentration within elite research networks (Marginson, 2006) diverts scholarly capacity away from broader institutions. Our results challenge assumptions that WCU policies uniformly benefit national systems (Hazelkorn, 2015), extending prior work on political reservation effects (Chattopadhyay & Duflo, 2004) and network spillovers (Beaman et al., 2012; Qin, Wang & Zhang, 2026). Policy implications emphasize differentiated support for research infrastructure alongside market-facing capacity building.

**Keywords**: global university rankings, spillover effects, regression discontinuity, higher education policy, world-class universities, internationalization
